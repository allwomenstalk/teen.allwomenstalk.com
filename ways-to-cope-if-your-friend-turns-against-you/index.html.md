# 7 Ways to Cope When Your Best Friend Turns on You

If your friend turns against you, it can be really upsetting and even bewildering. You may have no idea whatsoever what you did to upset them. Sometimes you've done nothing at all. It can happen when you're at high school, or when you're much older. When your best friend turns on you or someone you trusted suddenly gives you the cold shoulder, navigating the emotional fallout can feel overwhelming. Here's what to do if your friend turns against you and how to handle the situation with clarity and self-respect.

When friends turn on you, it helps to keep a quick reference in mind on how to respond depending on the situation:

| Situation | Immediate Action | Long-Term Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Misunderstanding | Communicate calmly | Clear up confusion |
| Sudden Cold Shoulder | Give them space | Evaluate the bond |
| Nasty Behavior | Ignore & act casual | Protect your peace |

## 1. What's the Problem

The first thing to do if your friend turns against you is find out what the problem is in their eyes. Without intending to, you may have unwittingly offended them. Once you know what's upset them you can try to put it right. If there's been a misunderstanding, you can clear it up. Reaching out once to ask if something is wrong shows maturity. You can also consult expert guides on [interpersonal communication and conflict resolution](https://www.verywellmind.com) to keep your conversation constructive. However, if your friend turned on you for no reason that you can identify, avoid pressing too hard right away.

## 2. Give Them Space

Your friend may need some space to get over whatever's upset them, before your friendship returns to normal. Give them some time, rather than bugging them to talk to you. That will make you look needy and irritate them. Taking a step back gives both of you room to process your feelings without worsening the tension. Remember that if you suspect a simple misunderstanding, you can always assess [what the problem might be](#1) once emotions have settled down.

## 3. Not True Friendship

If your friend persists in not speaking to you, or believes someone else over you, then it may be that your friendship wasn't really that strong to begin with. Many friendships end because one person gets the wrong impression over something, or even because another party lies. It may even end for no good reason at all. When friends who turn their back on you refuse to hear your side, it is often a sign of deeper underlying issues. But there's nothing you can do to make someone stay friends with you if they are unwilling to work through conflict.

Here are a few signs that a friendship may not have been built on a solid foundation:

- They listen to rumors without asking for your perspective.

- They drop the friendship unexpectedly without trying to communicate.

- They expect perfection from you while ignoring their own mistakes.

## 4. Ignore Them

If your former friend is being very juvenile and nasty, don't show any response or let them think that they've got to you. People can be very mean to someone they used to call their friend, and immature individuals may enlist other friends to pick on you. When someone turns others against you, giving into drama only fuels their actions. Don't give them the satisfaction; show that you're more mature than they are, and take absolutely no notice of their silly behavior. If you need support or advice dealing with peer pressure, organization resources like [Teen Line](https://teenline.org) offer helpful tools for young people.

## 5. Act Casual

Even grown adults can be very childish and mean towards their former friends. Teens can be even worse. If you're being picked on by your friend, act casually and shrug it off. So what if they're talking about you and laughing? As I've said, don't let them see that they're getting to you. They'll probably lose interest if they see that they aren't getting a reaction. Maintain your composure and focus on people who uplift you. Staying calm is much easier when you remember to [ignore their negative behavior](#4) entirely.

## 6. Ask Them to Stop

You could also tell them to stop their behavior if you think that it might make them listen to reason. If they don't want to be friends with you any more that's up to them, but there's no need to be childish and pick on you. If you make it clear that they're upsetting you, they may realise that they're being silly and stop. Direct and assertive communication is supported by experts at [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com) as a healthy way to establish firm boundaries when someone crosses the line.

## 7. Move on

Ultimately you may have to simply accept that the friendship has run its course and move on. It's upsetting if you don't know why your friend has turned against you, and sometimes you never know. It's probably something that you didn't even do. Accept that you're no longer friends, be sad for a while, then get on with your life. If you feel ready after taking time to process, you can focus on building positive connections with people who value you for who you are.

It probably hurts even more when a friend turns on you than when someone you know doesn't like you is mean to you. You feel a sense of betrayal and loss. But there's often nothing you can do except move on. Have you ever been dumped by a friend without knowing why?
