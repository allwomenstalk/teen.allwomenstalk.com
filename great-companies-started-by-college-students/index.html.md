# 7 Great Companies Started by College Students

As I personally start my own job search, I was interested to know if there were many companies started by college students out there. Turns out, college kids started some of the most successful companies in our market. Take a look at these companies started by college students and you might be inspired to start a business of your own. Many famous college companies began with just a simple idea to solve a student's everyday problem before growing into worldwide operations, proving that companies that target college students can quickly evolve into global market leaders.

## 1. Google

One of the most famous companies in the world is among the many companies started by college students. Larry Page and Sergey Brin founded Google in 1996 while they were both working towards their PhD at Stanford. They came up with the idea while writing a research project for their studies and originally called it “BackRub.” Years later, Google has transformed how humanity accesses information and remains the top search engine globally, accessible on the official [Google](https://www.google.com) site.

## 2. Reddit

When he was 22, Alexis Ohanian came up with the idea for Reddit while at the University of Virginia. He worked with Steve Huffman to create Reddit as the famous social networking site we know today. A year after it was founded in 2005, it was acquired by Condé Nast Publications and has grown tremendously over the years. Today, millions of users engage on [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com) every month. Unlike traditional search engines like [Larry Page and Sergey Brin's initial search tool](#1), Reddit relies on community-driven content aggregation and discussion.

## 3. Insomnia Cookies

What would the world be like without this late night cookie delivery service? I don’t want to imagine a world without Insomnia, and thanks to Seth Berkowitz, we don’t have to. Berkowitz started baking cookies while a junior at U-Penn in 2002. After so many people wanted some of his delicious late night cookies, he decided to turn it into a business. Today, you can order warm treats directly from [Insomnia Cookies](https://insomniacookies.com) across hundreds of campus locations. It stands as a prime example of companies that target college students by solving late-night cravings.

Several key factors contributed to Insomnia Cookies' rapid growth:

- Identifying a specific, unmet late-night student need
- Offering convenient dorm delivery when options were limited
- Building strong local brand loyalty on college campuses

## 4. Dell

Not many of the top computer companies in the world got their starts in a dorm room at the University of Texas-Austin. But Michael Dell didn’t start just any old company, he started Dell computers. He started his own business in 1984 by selling IBM PCs out of his dorm room and eventually created his own computer by 1985. Just a few years after that, Michael Dell was the youngest CEO of a Fortune 500 company. Today, tech hardware specs and products can be explored on the official [Dell](https://www.dell.com) website.

## 5. Facebook

We all know the Facebook story of how Mark Zuckerberg started the most popular social media site from his room at Harvard in 2004. But you might not know that it was initially started as a way for students to blog and connect. A decade after its inception, Facebook grew into a multi-billion dollar tech giant. Much like how [Alexis Ohanian's platform](#2) built online communities, Facebook redefined personal connection across the web.

## 6. WordPress

Where would the world of blogging be without the wonderful help of WordPress? It was founded by a freshman at the University of Houston, Matt Mullenweg, who wanted to make it easier to create websites. Today, power users and developers can download the open-source software at [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org). Unlike hardware-centric ventures like [Michael Dell's computer business](#4), WordPress focused entirely on democratizing web publishing for creators.

## 7. FedEx

Fred Smith came up with the idea for FedEx in the '60s while writing a paper for an economics class at Yale. It presented a plan of a delivery service that worked overnight. His professor gave him a “C.” The joke is on the professor because now FedEx is one of the top delivery companies in the nation and generates tens of billions of dollars annually. You can learn more about modern logistics services at [FedEx](https://www.fedex.com).
